# Responsive spy smoke (experimental)

An optional replacement for the spy smoke grenade, inspired by the responsive volumetric smoke in CS2. It is a match rule, disabled by default. The ordinary smoke grenade remains the default; enabling this rule changes both concealment and merc gas exposure for everyone in the round.

## Enabling and saving the rule

- In **Create a Session** or the lobby's **Settings → Rules → Extra rules**, enable **Responsive smoke (experimental)**. Only players allowed to control the lobby can change it; changes reset player readiness.
- A dedicated server's JSON config accepts `"responsiveSmoke": true`. Omitted values are false. The server console accepts `set responsivesmoke on` and `set responsivesmoke off`.
- Offline play and a command-line LAN host accept `--svm-responsive-smoke` with `--svm-play=Map`.
- Local TestRange launches automatically enable Practice resources. Explicit online sessions and recorded demos retain their authoritative rules.
- Host setup persistence and game/demo launch options use `?SvmResponsiveSmoke=0|1`. The rule is independent of Practice and Alternate rules and survives map/mode changes and rotation.
- The running round keeps the rule with which it started. A server-console change during a match configures the next round; the browser and match log continue to identify the active round's setting.
- The browser labels enabled servers with **responsive smoke**. Host lobby setup includes the option for every participant.

## Shared gameplay

Smoke expands into reachable space around its source, respecting solid map geometry. Openings, corridors and changing blockers constrain its shape. It grows and dissipates smoothly; overlapping clouds contribute to the same sampled smoke field.

Mercenary bullet rays, including individual shotgun pellets, clear thin paths through smoke. The Shocker, flashbangs and chaff do not clear it. Frag grenades and explosive mines (including mine chain reactions) clear a larger region through the common damaging-blast path; solid geometry constrains the affected cells. The density then recovers while the smoke source is still alive. Clearing smoke does not remove the emitter or extend its lifetime.

For a blast next to a floor or wall, visibility starts from the nearest reachable open voxel within 50 uu, checked by an unobstructed collision trace, then follows conservative voxel visibility. This lets a resting frag or mounted mine clear its side of a surface without moving the actual explosion center or enlarging its radius.

The initial tuning retains the existing 10-second smoke lifetime and 300 uu outer radius:

| Behavior | Initial value |
| --- | --- |
| Shared field | 25 × 25 × 25 cells, 25 uu per cell, aligned to the world |
| Growth front | 1200 uu/s, followed by a 0.10 s local density fade-in |
| Natural fade | Starts at 8 s; fully gone at 10 s |
| Bullet path | 12 uu clear radius, 6 uu soft edge; hold 0.10 s, refill over 0.40 s |
| Blast | Radius capped at 300 uu and the blast's damaging range; 25 uu soft edge; hold 0.60 s, refill over 1.40 s |
| Gas exposure | Mean density at seven breathing samples (eye and ±10 uu on each axis), threshold 0.25 |

Propagation uses connected open cells, including unobstructed diagonals; it cannot cut through solid edges or corners. Geometry checks conservatively test the whole cell against solid collision, and unclassified cells remain blocked until checked. This deliberately limits propagation through openings smaller than the cell size.

Initial classification extends one shortest-path front while preserving its unquantized path distances. It does not restart future arrivals at every geometry batch, which would slow expansion despite the configured growth speed. Newly published cells retain their local fade even if bounded classification discovers them after the front should have arrived, avoiding instantly dense voxel bands. Actual geometry invalidation still cancels future paths and rebuilds them from smoke already released into valid air. Classification and front processing retain fixed per-tick work budgets, so simultaneous clouds can take longer to reveal their full reachable region.

Partially blocked cells retain the exact intersecting collision surface planes. Air in these boundary cells borrows density from the nearest reached core cell only when both points remain on the same side of every surface in the complete cell supercover. This fills smoke up to floors, angled walls and corners without removing the wall between rooms; two sides of a thin wall can fill independently. Cells with more than eight distinct planes remain conservatively blocked. Fully open cells normalize interpolation over geometrically valid neighbors, retaining unreached air as zero density. Boundary cells do not carry the propagation front. These boundaries replicate in codec version 2, including sparse plane changes; the actor-extension signatures change the existing protocol hash.

When the grenade releases within 50 uu of supporting floor, its cloud envelope center is raised 140 uu while growth still begins at the actual release point. The outer radius remains 300 uu. Shared optical extinction is 0.060 per uu, including light self-shadowing. This makes short paths through the cloud body conceal geometry as well as full-diameter paths. Smoke already released into a room remains there if its door closes, fading on the original source lifetime.

The current shared envelope uses two scales of deterministic macro noise rather than a nearly spherical sine rim. `ResponsiveSmokeNoise` independently generates a periodic 64³ byte lattice, with eight cellular features per axis and eight texels per feature. Each feature is jittered by up to ±0.2 feature units around its cell center. A Worley clump value, `1 − smoothstep(0.12, 0.85, F1)` where `F1` is nearest-feature distance, contributes 85%; gradient Perlin noise contributes 15%. The renderer uploads the same bytes as an R8 volume. CPU and GPU use plain trilinear interpolation, with feature coordinate `q` mapped to texture coordinate `(q × 8 + 0.5) / 64`. Generation is lazy and explicitly warmed during enabled-match loading; disabled matches do not generate this table.

Both samplers evaluate the field relative to the cloud center and age. Three ordered, invertible sinusoidal shears move a persistent material pattern along curved paths, with an 18 uu/s upward drift. The radius, solid core, collision boundaries and clearing positions stay in world space. Inside 150 uu the envelope multiplier is 1; from 150 to 210 uu it blends toward a noise-shaped shell multiplier of 0.55–1. The broad band uses a 135 uu feature scale, remapped with `smoothstep(0.28, 0.72)`; a 55 uu band adds smaller edge variation. The outer edge is `240 + 54 × macro + 6 × fine` uu, bounded to 240–300 uu, with an 18 uu fade inward from that edge. Growth, natural fade and temporary clearings still multiply this envelope. This shape changes shared optical density, gas and AI queries equally, independent of graphics quality; it is separate from the visual-only flow below. The shape extension signature is `responsive-smoke-state-2-shape-5`, retaining wire codec version 2. These are OpenSCCT tuning values, not recovered CS2 formulas or copied assets, and visual parity has not been established.

Merc gas exposure and AI concealment queries use the same field that is sent to clients. A visually clear gap does not continue applying gas merely because it lies inside the original spherical radius. Existing gas-mask protection, cough linger, movement effects, blur and knockout timing remain governed by the original smoke rules in [smoke-grenade.md](../equipment/smoke-grenade.md). Spies retain their existing gas immunity. An already-started cough/blur can linger after leaving density, as it does when leaving ordinary smoke.

## Graphics and network contract

**Options → Display → Image quality → Responsive smoke quality** offers Low, Medium and High; Medium is the default. This setting changes rendering detail and performance only. It cannot disable smoke, alter the shared field, change clearing lifetime, or change gas/AI rules. The per-computer key is `responsive_smoke_quality` (0 Low, 1 Medium, 2 High); invalid numeric values recover to Medium.

The renderer integrates the shared field on the GPU into a cumulative volume for each view, then samples it at each surface fragment's own depth, including transparent surfaces, with a far-depth background pass for rays that reach the sky. The cache includes blast clearing. Rays intersecting bullet paths refine optical extinction at full pixel resolution. Surfaces inside a prefix depth interval also use exact extinction when the endpoint transmittance differs by more than 0.015; smaller endpoint spans bound the depth-interpolation contribution to error, excluding spatial filtering and integration error. This avoids making short smoke-to-surface paths too transparent, without interpolating optical depth across empty geometry or blast cavities. Refined rays retain cached scattering scaled by the corrected opacity, preserving small openings without a cloudy halo.

| Quality | Visual flow grid | View grid spacing | Depth slices | Base integration step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 16³ | 10 pixels | 16 | 12.5 uu |
| Medium | 32³ | 8 pixels | 32 | 8.33 uu |
| High | 48³ | 3 pixels | 64 | 6.25 uu |

Medium and High add illumination detail. These sampling choices affect visual approximation, not the physical field or temporary clearing shapes and times. Normal, night and motion vision use the shared smoke scattering; thermal uses one-fifth extinction, and EMF preserves its clear-smoke treatment. This integrates with the game's custom vision/material pipeline rather than enabling Godot's global volumetric fog.

`ResponsiveSmokeVisualFlow` adds persistent GPU transport for rolling illumination detail: semi-Lagrangian advection, curl forcing, gentle buoyancy, damping and bullet/blast velocity impulses. It uses eight pooled cloud slots with ping-pong RGBA16F volumes. Additional clouds retain the same authoritative optical field and use procedural lighting detail. This is a bounded visual approximation, without a pressure solve. It modulates scattering only; it cannot change optical extinction, gas exposure, geometry, the outer envelope or the lifetime and shape of a clearing.

The visual flow's initial scalar combines independent 110 uu and 45 uu value-noise bands with weights 0.75/0.25, then maps the result to `0.10 + 0.90 × noise`. Newly reached cells receive this source while existing detail is transported. Lighting blends 75% of the shared moving macro form with 25% transported detail remapped by `smoothstep(0.32, 0.72)`, then applies a 0.60–1 albedo multiplier. The major illuminated forms therefore move with the silhouette; transport adds smaller folds. High mixes 10% additional 55 uu material detail into its transported component. This quality-dependent detail cannot create a gameplay sight line.

The visual solver runs on a fixed 30 Hz smoke-time clock, with at most two steps per draw. Repeated views at the same time do not advance it again. Late joins, larger stalls, clock rewinds, replacement clouds and resolution changes reseed detail at the current age instead of building an unbounded catch-up queue. Impulses are keyed by sequence, creation time and kind so a repeated network snapshot cannot apply the same impulse twice. The flow shader clips advection and interpolation paths against authoritative occupied cells, including edge/corner crossings; newly blocked cells lose their stored velocity and detail.

Smoke lighting samples eight spatial probes across the cloud, each with its own map-region ambient and local light selection. Point, spot and directional illumination use mild Henyey–Greenstein forward scattering (`g = 0.22`) and short density-shadow samples. Map spotlights and the mercenary torch reuse their existing shadow/cookie atlas. These lighting inputs affect radiance only.

The probes span ±150 uu around the cloud center and project into reachable cells when a sample falls in solid geometry. Direct illumination uses the map's point-light selection at each probe. A 300 uu actor-volume light gather supplies bounded indirect support for authored rooms with zero zone ambient: it retains the broad gather's color, takes half of its excess maximum-channel energy above local direct illumination, caps that support at 0.22 display units, and fades it out as local direct intensity rises from 0.10 to 0.35. This approximates neighboring illumination; it is not a global illumination solve or a raised emissive floor. Light colors retain the existing renderer's display-space convention.

Before applying the visual lobe albedo, the shader compresses combined incident RGB by the single scalar `1 + 0.65 × max(R, G, B)`. This preserves its channel ratios while reducing the bright-probe clipping that erased cloud detail. A final 0.025–0.9 channel clamp remains. The compression changes radiance only, not extinction or the shared density shape; it does not recolor every map's smoke to match the warm lighting in the CS2 reference.

Direct scattering splits energy into 85% density-attenuated HG scattering and 15% isotropic diffuse support. Density shadowing samples the union of clouds over at most 255 uu with 1/2/3 samples on Low/Medium/High. Cubic segment boundaries concentrate the Medium/High samples near the visible lobe skin: each sample uses its segment midpoint and actual segment length, preserving the integral for uniform density. Broad blast openings transmit incident light; narrow bullet paths remain part of the full-pixel optical correction rather than the coarse shadow samples. Ordinary point lights retain their existing geometry-shadow limitations. Probe projection positions cache by cloud identity and geometry baseline revision, while light values refresh for moving/switched lights; bullet-only updates do not repeat cell searches.

Medium and High reproject cumulative scattered radiance into the previous view volume. Optical transmittance always comes from the current field and is never accumulated temporally. Camera/viewport/projection changes, quality or vision switches, geometry baselines, cloud replacement, cuts and clock discontinuities reject incompatible history; local opacity disagreement also rejects a reprojected sample. The full-pixel bullet correction remains active after this reconstruction. GPU transport and history use the global RenderingDevice and its resource barriers on both supported drivers.

`SmokeRenderCache` publishes immutable frames at the client tick boundary. Clouds retain a stable identity; copied cell/activation data, boundary planes and carve masks are reused until their `(Revision, SnapshotRevision)` stamp changes. The second revision ensures a delayed geometry baseline still redraws after a newer bullet event, including a boundary-only update with unchanged occupancy. Drawing reads this published frame rather than live simulation collections.

The authoritative round flag is `GameReplicationInfo.ResponsiveSmokeEnabled`. `ResponsiveSmokeWorld` owns geometry and gameplay queries; `ResponsiveSmokeField` evaluates growth, fade and displacement against server time. `ResponsiveSmokeReplication` sends complete and incremental field state plus a separate stream of temporary clearings. These travel on the replicated emitter's persistent actor channel, including the always-relevant recorder connection. Game/demo options record the rule. The state extension signature is `responsive-smoke-state-2-shape-5`: the existing protocol hash rejects builds or recordings using earlier density-shape, growth-ramp or optical-extinction semantics, even when their payload layout matches. This feature does not add mixed-version fallback.

Rendering requires Forward+ with the Vulkan or Direct3D 12 rendering driver. The `ResponsiveSmokeRenderer.UnsupportedReason` capability result identifies unsupported configurations and compute initialization failures. Disabled rounds use only inactive placeholder bindings. Enabled loading prepares the common field uploads, flow and view-volume textures by rendering private smoke frames at the gameplay viewport size, including actual map surface materials. A loading cover also handles rules arriving after initial loading; packets continue while input is gated. The CPU lattice can still initialize synchronously if a late rules-and-cloud packet is consumed before that presentation gate observes the flag.

Enabled idle periods retain bounded atlas capacity while dispatching no smoke GPU work. Cloud identities and lighting history reset before reuse; disabling the rule or removing the last view fully releases volume resources. Mature activation atlases are reused until geometry, time rewind, layout or resource changes require another upload. The view-volume pass skips integration outside each ray's conservative cloud bounds without changing depth slices, sampling quality or opacity. There is no personal option to fall back to ordinary smoke in an enabled match.

GPU exports keep stable RenderingServer texture identities. The view volume writes a fixed output and copies it into the history image; visual flow copies its ping-pong result into a fixed export image at its 30 Hz step rate. Assigning a new `TextureRdRid` each draw would recreate the RenderingServer texture and notify dependent materials, as shown in Godot's [TextureRD implementation](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/scene/resources/texture_rd.cpp). Copies are included in the GPU timestamps, and flow readback checks inspect the exported image consumed by both prefix and surface shaders. Flow storage now includes three bounded images; the impulse buffer is included in accounting and trims an exceptional allocation above 1 MiB back to its 64 KiB ordinary reserve on idle.

## Adapting the smoke research report

The supplied CS2-style research report informed the visual transport, geometry-aware sampling, spatial lighting, GPU pooling and temporal reconstruction. Its proposed values are engineering examples, not OpenSCCT requirements or verified CS2 internals. This implementation retains the approved **10-second lifetime, 300 uu outer radius, default-off match rule, existing grenade fuse and gas rules, and bullet/damaging-blast-only clearing scope**. It does not adopt the report's longer lifetime, altered shotgun radii, new fire/decoy/C4 interactions or different gameplay visibility thresholds.

The implementation targets this project's Godot **4.7.2** runtime and existing Forward+ material/vision pipeline, with Vulkan and Direct3D 12 support. It does not downgrade to the report's suggested engine version or substitute Godot FogVolume/particle fallback behavior in an enabled match. The report's performance budgets are targets to compare against, not measurements of this implementation; validation reports the actual local fixture and hardware separately.

## Verification

Required checks cover default-off behavior, saved and launch-option round trips, host permissions, next-round changes, late joins and demo seeking, and matching field queries for gas, AI and rendering. Visual checks cover enclosed rooms, narrow openings, moving doors, overlapping clouds, camera entry/exit, shot trails, blast recovery and each vision mode. Measure CPU/GPU cost with simultaneous clouds and repeated firing before making performance claims. Geometry is a conservative voxel approximation, and openings smaller than its cell size are not guaranteed to admit smoke.
