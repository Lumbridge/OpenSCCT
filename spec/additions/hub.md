# The Hub (an OpenSCCT addition)

The hub is not part of Splinter Cell Chaos Theory Versus or of any of its patches. It is our own design: a social
space for up to about 50 players, in the spirit of a game's park, plaza or arcade lobby, where spies and mercs walk
around with the full moveset, see each other's names, chat and, in later phases, start matches and play minigames.
Nothing here is a port, so no rule carries a `[PortOf]` or a provenance tag; the numbers are our own choices and
live in `OpenScct.Server.Hub.HubK`, `DistanceInterestSettings` and `ProximityVoiceSettings`. Everything below the
hub (pawns, controllers, moves, traversal, inventory, replication of each actor) is the ported original.

Phases: H1 server, scaling, avatars, chat, names and positional voice; H2 match pads (rules 19 to 26);
H3 minigames and leaderboards; H4 polish.

## Rules

1. **The map.** The hub runs on the Hub map, generated by `tools/hubmap` (our own geometry, tracked in
   `fixtures/hub`). Its areas are in `fixtures/hubmap/areas.json`: the spawn plaza (64 starts, spies on the west half
   of two rings round the fountain, mercs on the east half), the traversal garden (the TestRange station kit at the
   TestRange heights: ledges 128 to 320, wall jump 408, pipe 512, ladder 320, split shaft 192, vault 96, fence 376, hand
   over hand 277, curb, step, stairs, a ramp to a deck with a tower and a 1760 uu zip line), the match pads (H2, rules 19 to 26: four
   railed courts with sign gantries and bleachers, each wearing a swappable theme kit), the merc aim range (H3: a
   booth of five stalls, a berm with painted targets, a leaderboard), the spy movement course (H3: a numbered route of
   vault, climb, fence, hand over hand, pipe or ladder and zip line, a leaderboard) and the lounge (seats, a screen).
   The look (night rooftop, signage, theme kits) is described in docs/HUB.md "The map"; it changes nothing in play.
2. **The match never ends.** The server runs the SBase game rules as a Story match with no time limit and lives that
   never run out (`HubK.GameOptions`), started before anyone joins (`SHubGameInfo.StartHub`). The end checks never
   run: a leaver is treated as a spectator for the end check, a death scores nothing and ends nothing, and the map has
   no objectives.
3. **Joining.** A player joins directly, with no lobby, while the match runs, and always plays (never the late
   joiner's spectator seat of the original's login rule). He gets the side he asked for (`?Team=0` spies, `?Team=1`
   mercs; anything else is spies), whatever the team sizes, up to the server's player limit (50 by default; the SBase
   cap of 32 does not apply). His name is cleaned (printable, at most 20 characters, no `?` or `#`) and made unique
   with a number. The server says `hub welcome`, his side and the message of the day, and tells everyone he joined.
4. **Spawning.** Every spawn is the original's `RestartPlayer` at a start of the player's side: the start farthest from
   every pawn, so 50 players spread over the 64 starts. The side's pawn class, field of view and default kit are the
   original's.
5. **Side change and respawn.** `#svm hub side <0|1>` moves the player to that side and respawns him at the plaza as
   that side; `#svm hub respawn` puts him back at the plaza. Either at most once every 2 s; a refusal is a system line.
   The old pawn goes at once; the new one is spawned once the player's client has let go of the old (its channel's close
   is acknowledged, at most 3 s), so the client's restart waits for the new pawn instead of restarting the old one.
   A spy who turns merc has his crouch toggle cleared (only a spy's start state sets it).
6. **No damage, no attacks.** The level's `PlayRules` service turns off all damage (every `PawnTakeDamage` on the
   server is dropped before any reaction, so no hit reactions, no stun, no deaths) and every pawn-on-pawn attack (a pawn
   can never be grabbed, elbow or melee shocked or drop attacked). Clients install the same rules, so none starts an
   attack its server refuses. Shooting, gadgets and every move still work.
7. **Back at the plaza.** A player whose pawn is gone, dead or below z = -2048 is spawned again after 3 s.
8. **Text chat.** The original's chat relay (`ChatRelay`, spec/hud/chat.md): Say reaches everyone in the hub, TeamSay
   the player's side, with the original's 260 characters a second for all senders together. System lines (joins,
   leaves, the message of the day, refusals) are `#svm hub msg` lines.
9. **Names over heads.** Every client draws the name of each other player's pawn within 4500 uu, 110 uu over the
   pawn's location, coloured by side (`HubClient.CollectNameTags`), from the pawn's replicated
   PlayerReplicationInfo.
10. **Session lines.** On the control channel, after the online session's prefix: server to client `hub welcome
    <version> <yourId> <maxPlayers> <name>`, `hub side <team>`, `hub msg <text>`; client to server `hub side <team>`,
    `hub respawn` (`HubProtocol`).

## Scaling (interest management)

11. **Opt-in.** `NetDriverSettings.Interest` is null in every normal match, which replicates exactly as before. The hub
    sets a `DistanceInterest`, and a per-connection send budget (`SendBudgetMode.PerConnection`): the game's one shared
    budget would split one client's rate between 50 clients.
12. **What it decides.** For each candidate actor of a viewer's pass, after the owner test and before the frequency
    gate: the actor's anchor (itself for a pawn; for anything else the pawn it is based on or owned by, or a
    controller's pawn) against the viewer's view point. The viewer's own actors are never touched. Always-relevant
    actors (the game state, every player's replication info) update at most every 0.5 s. Level actors are never
    culled.
13. **Bands.** Inside 2500 uu the viewer's 4 nearest other pawns, and what they carry, replicate as in a match; the
    rest of a crowd inside 2500 uu, and everything from 2500 uu, updates at most every 0.5 s; from 4500 uu every
    0.75 s, from 6500 uu every 1 s; beyond 7000 uu an actor with no channel is not relevant, and an open channel is kept until
    8000 uu, then closes after the stock relevancy timeout (5 s). A deferred update keeps its changes dirty for the
    next allowed pass.
14. **Proxy samples.** The per-viewer position samples of other pawns (spec/native/net-proxy.md) keep the decoded
    schedule for the 4 nearest; for the others the minimum and forced intervals are scaled (10x in the crowd band, 14x
    and 18x farther out), forced samples included, and an action batch that waits goes with the next sample.

## Proximity voice

15. **Server.** `ProximityRelayFilter.CanHear(listener, talker)`: both have a position and are within 3000 uu (3400
    while the pair is already in range), and the host has not muted the talker for that listener. It only narrows what
    the stock voice routing allows. The relay sends a talker's frames only to listeners it allows.
16. **Client.** `ProximityListener`: full volume within 400 uu, a smooth step down to silence at 3000 uu, times 0.45
    and a 1200 Hz low-pass through a wall (a line check), times the player's own volume for that talker (0 to 2),
    silent when he muted him; gain and cut-off ease toward their targets at 8 per second.
17. **Voice mode.** The hub's voice chat is Open: spies and mercs hear each other on the "all" channel, the team
    channel reaches the talker's side only, and the distance rule (15) narrows both. Positions are each living pawn's
    view point (location plus eye height); a player with no pawn is neither heard nor hears. The listener's ear on
    his own client is his camera.
18. **Keys and settings.** Push to talk, voice activation, the microphone mute and the voice volume are the player's
    voice settings, as in a match; the voice keys are not read while he types a chat line.

## Activities (H3): things to do while waiting

Our own small games for the time between matches, each with a score board people can show off on. The server
decides everything: it watches the pawns, keeps the clocks, raises the targets, rolls the ball and scores; clients
only draw what it says (`HubActivities` on the server, `HubPlayState` on the client, the `hubplay` lines of
`HubPlayProtocol`). The numbers are in `HubPlayK`; where the areas are comes from the map's manifest
(`HubLayout`, rule A12).

A1. **Course gates.** A gate is a disc on the floor: a pawn is over it when its centre is within the gate's radius
    on the floor and between 192 uu below and 320 uu above the gate's height. The start gate is 192 uu across the
    middle, the finish 256.
A2. **A run.** Only spies run (a merc in the start gate is told the course is for spies). The clock starts the moment
    a spy who stood in the start gate steps out of it. Checkpoints must be passed in order; the finish counts only
    once all of them are passed (otherwise the runner is told which one he missed). Going back into the start gate
    resets the run; it is cancelled by a respawn or side change, by going more than 512 uu outside the course's area,
    after 300 s, or with X. The time is the server's, in milliseconds, from the tick he left the start to the tick
    he reached the finish.
A3. **Checkpoints.** The course's own `checkpoints` list if the manifest has one (radius 640); else one checkpoint
    over the middle of each of the course's traversal pieces (`ges`, in the manifest's order, radius 256), so each
    piece has to be climbed or crossed; else three on the line from start to finish (radius 640).
A4. **Aim range rounds.** A merc at the firing line (at most 96 uu in front of it, anywhere behind it in the range)
    presses U to start a 30 s round; at most four rounds run at once, one per merc. Each round keeps two targets up
    at random places between 256 uu in front of the backstop and 512 uu short of the firing line, across the lane;
    a target stays up 1.6 to 3.2 s unless hit, and the next one rises 0.35 s after one falls. Leaving the range by
    more than 256 uu, a respawn, a side change or X cancels the round (no score).
A5. **Targets and hits.** A target is two boxes facing the firing line: a body 44 uu wide and 128 high, and over it,
    after a 4 uu gap, a head 26 wide and 30 high, both 12 deep. The merc's rifle shoots as in a match (its spread,
    its trace); on the hub server the trace also tests the raised targets, and the nearer of a target and whatever
    else the bullet met is hit. A target is not a pawn, so the hub's no-damage rule (rule 6) is untouched: the hit
    only counts, through the hub-only `PlayRules.RangeTargetsTakeHits`. A hit counts only on the shooter's own
    targets; every rifle shot during his round counts as a shot.
A6. **Range score.** 10 points for a body hit, 25 for a headshot, plus an accuracy bonus at the end: 50 times the
    share of shots that hit, rounded. The shooter sees the clock, points, hits, shots and headshots live.
A7. **Boards.** Three boards, each the top 10 of every player's best by name: the course (fastest time), the range
    (highest round score; shown with accuracy and headshots) and the plaza ball (most goals). A tie goes to whoever
    set it first. The player is told his time or score, whether it is his personal best and his place. A new first
    place sends a fanfare (a chime and a notice) to the players within 3500 uu and a line to everyone's chat. The
    tables are kept in a small JSON file next to the hub server (`hub-scores.json`), so they survive a restart.
    Every board text goes through `HubActivities.PublishBoard(id, text)`.
A8. **Emotes.** G opens the emote menu (1 wave, 2 salute, 3 cheer, 4 dance, 5 sit, 6 point, 7 laugh, 8 clap;
    Escape closes) and `/wave` and the like in the chat box do the same. The server lets a player emote at most
    every 1.5 s and tells the players within 4500 uu, who show it under his name for 4 s (8 s to dance or sit).
A9. **The jukebox.** A box in the plaza's north-east corner plays one of four procedural tracks of our own; it is
    heard within 1600 uu, at full volume within 384 uu. U within 384 uu puts on the next track for everyone (at most
    every 4 s). The music is made by the client's synthesiser, so there is no recorded audio and no licence to track.
A10. **The plaza ball.** A ball (radius 24) lies south of the fountain. A pawn walking into it kicks it away from
    him at 1.6 times his speed (at least 320, at most 1400 uu/s); it loses 0.9 of its speed per second, bounces off
    the plaza's edges and the fountain (0.7 of its speed kept), and stops below 12 uu/s. The west and east edges have
    a goal 576 uu wide in the middle: the ball through one is a goal for the last player who kicked it, and the ball
    is put back after 2 s. The server sends the ball on a kick and when it stops to everyone, and every 0.1 s while
    it rolls to the players within 6000 uu.
A11. **Session lines.** Server to client: `hubplay board|mark|run|range|target|emote|jukebox|ball|toast|fanfare`;
    client to server: `hubplay emote <name>`, `hubplay use` (U), `hubplay cancel` (X). The fields are in
    `HubPlayProtocol`. A board's lines are joined with `|`; a line over 500 bytes loses rows from the end.
A12. **Where.** From `fixtures/hubmap/areas.json` (next to the map's folder, or `HubSettings.AreasPath`): the course's
    `start`, `finish`, `ges` or `checkpoints`, and its area; the range's `firingLine`, `backstop`, `lane` and area; the
    plaza's area; an area's `leaderboardAnchor` for its board, and the plaza's `jukebox`. Anything missing has a
    default next to its activity.
## Match pads (H2)

19. **Pads.** The hub map's `MatchPads` area holds the pads (Pad1 to Pad4): a floor rectangle each, an optional sign
    anchor and floor height. Each pad queues for one match, read from the hub config (`fixtures/hubmap/hub.json`):
    map code, mode, players per side (Merc Deathmatch: players in all, every one a merc), whether bots fill empty
    places and how many people start the countdown then. The mode must be one the map catalog says the map plays,
    else the map's default mode is used; a map the server does not have makes the pad unavailable.
20. **Standing on a pad.** A player whose living pawn is inside a pad's rectangle, between 64 uu under and 400 uu over
    its floor, is queued on it, after everyone already there. He leaves the queue when his pawn leaves the rectangle,
    dies, or he leaves the hub.
21. **Lineup.** Going through the queue in order, each player takes a place on the side he plays in the hub if it has
    room, else on the other side if it has room, else he waits for a later match.
22. **Ready and countdown.** Each player in the lineup readies up with U on the pad (U again unreadies); a player
    outside the lineup cannot be ready, and one pushed out of it loses his ready. When the lineup has as many people
    as the pad needs (every place, or the pad's minimum when bots fill) and all of them are ready, a countdown runs
    (10 s by default); it stops as soon as the lineup has fewer or anyone in it unreadies. The sign shows "Ready n/m"
    and "Waiting for n players to ready up (U)". A start that failed waits 10 s before the next countdown.
23. **Starting.** At the countdown's end the hub opens an online session for the lineup: its own UDP port from the
    config's range (28890 to 28897 by default, never the hub's), the pad's map and mode as its setup, one game, bots
    as the pad says, and a new random password as the join token. Each lineup player is sent `hub match <pad> <port>
    <team> <token> <map> <mode> <host|->` and leaves the queue; the others keep their places and the pad goes on.
    The session lets its players in on the given sides, has them ready on joining, and launches when all of them
    came, or 20 s after the first came.
24. **Back to the hub.** A player's visit to the match ends when its results arrive (after the end banner), when he
    leaves it, or when its connection fails. He then joins the hub again with `?HubReturn=<pad>`, and 1.5 s after
    spawning is moved beside that pad, off it. The session closes when its players left after the round, 45 s after
    the round ended, or 45 s after it opened if nobody came. A player sent to a match who stays in the hub is not
    queued again for 30 s.
25. **Signs.** The hub server may put text in the world: an id, a position, the text (lines split by a line break, at
    most 360 characters), a colour and a size. A player is sent a sign when his pawn comes within 7000 uu of it, its
    changes while within 8000 uu, and its removal when he goes farther or it is taken down. Clients draw signs facing
    the camera. Each pad has a sign `Pad.<id>`: "PAD n", the map's name and format, the lineup per side and the state
    ("Stand here to play", "Waiting for 1 merc", "Starting in 7...", "Match starting!"), coloured by state.
26. **Pad lines.** Every player is sent each pad's match and state when he joins and whenever either changes: `hub pad
    <id> <state> <seconds> <map> <mode> <teamSize> <spies> <spySlots> <mercs> <mercSlots> <loX> <loY> <hiX> <hiY>
    <floorZ> <title>` (state 0 off, 1 idle, 2 waiting, 3 counting down, 4 starting). A player on a pad is sent his
    own place as `hub queue <pad> <side> <text>`, and `hub queue - -1` when he is on none. Signs are `hub sign <id> <x>
    <y> <z> <RRGGBBAA> <scale> <text>` (`\n` a line break, `\\` a backslash) and `hub unsign <id>`.
